Match night.

Slide 2 ended on a rule: when the problem gets big, turn the thinking up. Tonight is the perfect test. Picture it: the World Cup final kicks off in a few hours, and nobody on Earth knows how it ends.

A different kind of question

Who wins tonight?

You cannot answer that from memory; there is nothing to remember, it has not happened. But you are not answering from nothing, either. Here is the form guide.

Tonight The favorite
  • won every match on the way here
  • eleven goals scored, one conceded
  • full squad fit
  • five days' rest
Tonight The underdog
  • knocked out the defending champions
  • the tournament's in-form goalkeeper
  • has trailed in every knockout match, and won each one
  • four days' rest

Wherever you landed, watch where the answer came from. Not a pop; there is no fact of the matter yet, anywhere, in any head. You weighed facts against each other: form against momentum, a rested squad against a hot goalkeeper. That is reasoning, and on this question it is the only tool there is. Give it more time and the call gets sharper, never certain: replay their semifinals, weigh the midfields, picture that keeper against ten shots. Thinking pays.

Breaking, an hour before kickoff

The favorite's star striker pulled up injured in the warm-up, and is out of the match.

One line of the form guide just died. Think again: who wins now?

Stick or switch, you just did it again: took one brand-new fact and rebuilt the whole call around it. Nothing got recalled; there is still nothing to recall. New fact in, sharper call out. Thinking pays again.

One more question

Who won the 2010 World Cup final?

Zero steps, zero effort; it was simply there. And notice: thinking harder would not have made it arrive any better, or any faster. That was a lookup, not a build.

Now sit with that for a second. An hour would not help. There is nothing to reason from: no form guide, no team sheets, nothing to weigh. The result is a fixed fact of history, and a fixed fact is either already in your head, or it is nowhere.

An agent is in exactly the same spot. Hand it tonight's form guide and the injury news, and thinking time turns into a sharper forecast. Ask it about a final it never saw, and an hour of thinking buys nothing; there is nothing to work from.

Put the whole evening next to slide 2's multiplication, and the map draws itself. Every answer you have ever given came off one of two shelves:

Memorized
"I've seen this one."
The answer pops out whole. Zero steps, zero time. It only works for things that have already crossed your path.
Understood
"I can rebuild it."
Steps take time. But it works on things nobody has met yet: a fresh multiplication, tonight's final. If the pieces exist, reasoning builds the best answer they allow.
And the 2010 final marks the boundary between them. It cannot be rebuilt: no pieces, nothing to reason from. It can only be remembered. On one kind of question, thinking is the whole game and turning it up keeps paying. On this kind, thinking buys nothing: the answer is either already in there, or it is nowhere.

Which leaves the real question. How does an answer get in there in the first place? That is a different machine from the one you have been watching, with its own name and its own price tag. Next slide.
