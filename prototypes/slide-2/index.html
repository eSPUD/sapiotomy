The machine's two speeds.

The game ended on a question: what if something answered instantly, no matter how big the problem gets? There is such a thing. Ask an AI agent to answer with no thinking allowed, and the answer just arrives: no steps, no pause, no scratch work. Let's find out what that is worth.

Before the last one, a prediction

Three for three, and the third one beat your best time by minutes. Next up is a size the game never reached: 4502 × 6398. Does the blurt hold?

Nothing on the surface changed. No hesitation, no "probably", no smaller font. It handed over a wrong number with exactly the confidence of the three right ones. And the miss is close: within four hundredths of a percent, near enough to sail past anyone not checking. That is the trouble with a pop: it is a guess wearing certainty's clothes, and it does not know when it is wrong.

So give the agent what round 3 of the game gave you: permission to think. Same problem, thinking allowed. Here is the reasoning trace, its actual scratch work, one step at a time.

Answer: 4502 × 6398 = 28,803,796 The blurt said 28,793,796. Six steps of checking caught it.

Notice what the trace did. It opened with the very same pop, then refused to trust it: broke the problem into pieces, worked each piece, hit a mismatch, and checked again by a different route. The instant answer is still in there; thinking is what catches it when it is wrong. More steps, more time, and the answer flipped from wrong to right.

Honesty note

In day-to-day use, a modern agent asked for arithmetic will usually just grab a calculator: one line of code, print(4502 * 6398), done. Arithmetic is this deck's demo because you can feel every step of it in your own head. The two speeds are real and they are everywhere; the multiplication is just the cleanest place to watch them.

So: the pop is fast and silent about its failures, and thinking catches them. A fair rule so far: when the problem gets big, turn the thinking up. Does that rule always hold? Tonight happens to offer the perfect test. Next slide: match night.
