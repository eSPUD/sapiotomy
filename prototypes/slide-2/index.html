That's the difference between knowing the answer because you memorized it (you "trained" on it) and understanding it (you can reason your way there). Now try a different kind of question

Who won the last World Cup final?

It just popped in, didn't it? That's memory again: you were there for the data, so to speak. No reasoning happened.

And here's the kicker: you can't reason your way to it. There's no concept to master that derives a match result, the way multiplication derives 223,300. You'd have to look it up: find a source, then reason with what it says.

An agent is in the same spot. If the match happened after its training, the winner simply isn't in its memory, and no amount of thinking alone can produce it. It has to reach for a source and reason with what it finds. But 350 × 638? That it can always rebuild, because the concept is enough.

These two ways of knowing have names in the world of AI, and the difference between them decides how machines get smarter. Next: what they're called, and what each one costs.
