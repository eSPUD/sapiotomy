Instant answer vs reasoning.

In the game, bigger problems meant more steps, and more steps meant more time. Let's look closer at what those steps actually are: reasoning.

For reference, here is a reasoning trace: how an agent works through the big one from the game, 350 × 638, one step at a time.

Answer: 350 × 638 = 223,300

Now, if someone asked you 350 × 638 again tomorrow, you wouldn't redo any of those steps. You've met this exact problem twice now (the game, then the trace), so the answer would just pop: 223,300. Zero steps. That pop is memory, not reasoning.

And that's the difference between knowing the answer because you memorized it and understanding it (you can reason your way there). Even when memory fades, you can always rebuild 223,300, because you've mastered the concept: multiplication, and breaking a big problem into steps.

Now a different kind of question

Picture it: the World Cup final is tonight. Who wins?

Whichever you picked, notice where it came from. It didn't pop out of memory; there's nothing to remember, the match hasn't happened. You reasoned: recent form, the matchup, who's under pressure. And the longer you thought, the sharper the call. Thinking time was buying you something.

Breaking, minutes before kickoff

The favorite's star striker just went off injured in the warm-up.

Think again: who wins now?

Stick or switch, you just did it again: took a brand-new fact and reasoned it into a better answer. The old call didn't get recalled, it got rebuilt. More thinking, still paying.

One more

Who won the 2010 World Cup final?

Zero steps, zero effort; it was just there. And notice: thinking harder wouldn't have made it arrive any better.

Here's the thing: you could sit here for an hour and it still wouldn't come. There's nothing to reason from. A fixed result is either already in your head, or it's nowhere.

An agent is in the same spot. Give it tonight's matchup and that injury report, and thinking time turns into a sharper call. Ask it about a final it never saw, and an hour of thinking buys nothing; there's nothing to work from.

Two kinds of question, then. On one, thinking is the whole game, and turning it up keeps paying. On the other, thinking buys you nothing; the answer is either already in there, or it isn't. So how does an answer get in there in the first place? That's a different machine, with its own name and its own price tag. Next slide.
