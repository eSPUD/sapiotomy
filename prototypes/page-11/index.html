It's $1.44. Eighteen months ago, the top score on the previous ARC-AGI cost thousands of dollars per task — for a lower score. The price of frontier thinking didn't just fall. It cratered — roughly a thousand-fold.
But the shape never left. Spend less, get less — the same family runs $0.67 → 59.5% (Luna), $1.09 → 83.9% (Terra), $1.44 → 92.5% (Sol): the last 8.6 points cost nearly as much as the previous 24. And paying more doesn't buy more — Gemini 3.1 Pro costs $40 a task for 77.1%, thirty times Terra's price for a worse score.
So is reasoning solved and cheap? The field already answered. On ARC-AGI-3 — the newest test — that same best model scores just 7.8%. Humans score 100%. The frontier moved, and the next bill is already forming.
The honest answer to page 1. Is machine thinking worth buying? Astonishingly, yes — the price collapsed a thousand-fold in eighteen months. But you still pay for the last points, you can still overpay, and the moment one benchmark falls, a harder one takes its place. The absolute price falls; the shape of the trade does not.
