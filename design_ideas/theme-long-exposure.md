# Theme: The Long Exposure

*The night sky, and how long you leave the shutter open on it. An evolution of the constellation
identity: same sky, but the theme becomes the camera, not the stars.*

## Abstract (250 words max)

Open the shutter. At first the frame holds only the bright, easy stars: the answers any camera
catches instantly. But leave the shutter open and the sky begins to accumulate. Photons that left
some distant furnace of plasma before there were pyramids land on the sensor one by one, and
structure no glance could ever see, spiral arms, nebulae, the dust lanes between them, rises slowly
out of the noise. Exposure is thinking. The longer the machine holds a question open, the more
signal it gathers, and the detail that emerges was always there, waiting for enough time.

The physics is honest about the price. Signal grows as the square root of time, so every extra
minute buys less than the minute before. And the sky punishes greed. Hold the shutter too long and
the Earth's own turning smears the stars into trails, the sensor's heat invents stars that were
never there, a satellite scrawls across the frame. More time, past its moment, ruins the picture.

The remedies are the toolbox. Take many short exposures instead of one long one. Stack them, and
let the votes cancel the noise. Reject the corrupted frames before they poison the stack. And mind
the meter: every hour of aperture is billed, and committees decide which targets deserve the
telescope.

This is inference-time scaling told in light: patience gathers truth, the gains thin as they grow,
and knowing when to close the shutter is part of seeing.

## Why this theme

- **All three legs hold.** Accumulation (photons = signal = accuracy), accumulation-as-process
  (the image literally develops over exposure time), and the backfire (trails, drift, thermal
  noise, streaks) are all real physics, not decoration. The inverse-U is native.
- **Diminishing returns come free.** Signal-to-noise grows as the square root of exposure time;
  the flattening curve is the actual law of the instrument, matching the deck's plateaus without
  hand-waving.
- **The toolbox is real practice.** Astrophotographers genuinely stack many short frames
  (self-consistency voting), sigma-clip outlier frames (verifier selection), and lose whole stacks
  to one bad reference frame (verifier betrayal).
- **The economics beat lands literally.** Telescope time is metered and billed by the hour;
  allocation committees are the compute-optimal allocation game.
- **Continuity, not repudiation.** The constellation assets, dark canvas, and amber-on-black
  palette survive; the story the visuals tell changes from star-pictures to exposure and
  accumulation. The proposal's existing telescope move for field data becomes a unified motif.

## Visual language

- **Palette:** near-black sky canvas; amber compute hue as the exposure/aperture color; accuracy
  as emerging star-white; error as streak-red. Matches the app's existing tokens.
- **Motifs:** shutter iris opening on section reveals; a frame that grains-in as budget rises;
  star-trail smears when a knob passes the peak; contact-sheet grids for sampling beats; an
  exposure meter as the persistent budget rail.
- **Motion:** slow accumulation fades (signal rising out of noise) in response to reader action
  only; reduced motion swaps to instant developed frames.
